Read the short hand first

The clock face carries two scales on the same twelve numbers, which is what makes it hard at first. For the hour hand — the short, thick one — the numbers mean what they say. For the minute hand — the long one — each number means five minutes, so the 9 at the left means 45. Read the short hand, say the hour out loud, and only then read the long hand.

The rule that catches almost everyone: the hour hand counts the number it has passed, never the number it is nearest. At 4:50 the short hand is a whisker away from the 5, but the hour is still 4 — the time is ten to five. Saying "it has gone past the 4, so it is four-something" before looking at the minute hand fixes this error faster than any amount of extra practice.

The five stages, in order

  1. O'clock. The minute hand points straight up at 12. Only the short hand carries information, so this is where the hour-hand habit gets built.
  2. Half hours. The minute hand points down at 6, and the hour hand now sits visibly between two numbers — the first time the "passed, not nearest" rule matters.
  3. Quarter hours. Quarter past, half past, quarter to. This is where the language of time arrives, and where "to" reverses the direction of counting.
  4. Five minutes. Count round the dial in fives. Knowing the five times table makes this stage fast; not knowing it makes this stage the wall.
  5. To the minute. Count on from the nearest number using the small ticks — 4:38 is "past the 7, plus three ticks".

Do not skip a stage because the child can chant the answer. The test is a clock they have not seen before, read cold, without counting from 12 each time. That is what the random generator above is for — and the reason each printed sheet is different from the last.

Using the two sheet types

Read the clock checks recognition and is the natural place to start. Draw the hands is harder and far more revealing: a child who can read 3:45 but draws the hour hand pointing straight at the 3 has not yet understood that it moves. Alternating the two — one of each per session — catches that gap early. Both print at twelve problems on one page, with the key on a separate sheet so it can be kept back or handed over.

Frequently asked questions

How do you read an analog clock?

Short hand for the hour, counting the number it has already passed. Long hand for the minutes, counting five per number. Short hand just past 3, long hand on 9 → 3:45.

What does "quarter to" mean?

Fifteen minutes before the next hour. Quarter to four is 3:45. "Past" counts forward from the hour just gone, "to" counts backward from the hour coming.

Why is the hour hand between two numbers?

It moves continuously — half a degree per minute — so by half past it sits midway between them. It only points exactly at a number on the hour.

At what age do children learn this?

Roughly: whole and half hours at five to six, quarter hours and five-minute intervals at seven, to the nearest minute at eight or nine. Order matters more than age.

Which hand is which?

The hour hand is shorter and thicker. The minute hand is longer and reaches nearly to the rim. If they overlap, the one whose tip is closer to the edge is the minute hand.
